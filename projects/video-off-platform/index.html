Facebook · Video Off-Platform

Turning off-platform video into an acquisition channel for Facebook

I improved the web experience for people who visited Facebook for video, then encouraged them to open the app or log in when they wanted to continue.

Problem

Facebook on web was a walled garden. This made it hard for unfamiliar users to understand its value

Video was a major content surface, but logged-out visits were shaped around access controls. That made it difficult for unfamiliar people to understand Facebook’s value before being asked to log in or open the app.

Context

Treat the off-platform visit as an acquisition stack

The visit was not one page or one prompt. It was a sequence of layers, and each layer had a different job before the next one could work.

Conversion
Logged-out page

Solution and ownership

Give each visit value before asking for more

I worked across the logged-out page and its conversion moments, deciding what the experience should help someone do first and which next step made sense on mobile and desktop web.

Make the logged-out page useful first

People arrived for a specific video. The page needed to make that visit worthwhile before Facebook asked them to change context, authenticate, or install anything.

Protect the viewing moment

Keep the video and its immediate context available instead of turning the visit into an access gate.

Make the next step understandable

Explain what logging in or opening the app adds so the action has clear value to the visitor.

Design the next ask across mobile and desktop web

I audited existing prompts, proposed experiment directions, and developed patterns for app entry, saved-account login, and reauthentication. The goal was not one universal prompt; it was a useful path for the visitor’s current context.

Use web login as a bridge

Saved-account entry and reauthentication gave returning visitors a lower-friction way back without forcing the strongest ask first.

Use app entry when the value is strongest

Opening the app remained the most ambitious outcome, so it belonged where continuing there offered a clear benefit.

How I framed the decision

Choose both the outcome and the force of the ask

Two ladders were shaping the same decision. The incrementality ladder defined what Facebook wanted someone to do. The aggressiveness ladder defined how forcefully the interface asked. Combining them made the tradeoffs visible in one model.

Where it landed

New metrics and a framework for conversion

The work expanded success beyond app opens. Web login became a conversion metric too because it gave returning visitors a lower-friction bridge into Facebook. The combined framework made both the outcome and force of each ask explicit.

Metric wins

App opens remained the strongest acquisition outcome, while web login became a tracked conversion rather than a secondary interaction.

Product framework

The combined incrementality and aggressiveness model gave teams a reusable way to compare prompts without collapsing every visit into “ask” or “do not ask.”

Reflection

Show the value, then ask at the right moment

Make the value of the action clear

People should understand what opening the app or logging in unlocks. The prompt works when the benefit is concrete, not when it only names the action.

Reach people in the right context

The same ask can help or interrupt depending on when it appears. I learned to place it after the visitor had received enough value for the next step to make sense.
